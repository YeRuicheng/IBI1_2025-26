The Practical 9 about stochastic SIR models is very interesting and helpful, but also challenging.

The basic SIR model is easy to implement, but understanding the probability calculation of infection takes some time. 
The SIR_vaccination makes herd immunity visualized, which helps me understand it intuitively.
The spatial SIR model is more challenging, especially dealing with 8-neighbor infection and boundary problems (although I checked the codes posted on BB).

Overall, I learned how to convert real epidemic problems into probabilistic models, and use Python for simulation and visualization. 
Most importantly, the stochastic models I learnt this week can show different results each time, which is closer to the real world.
